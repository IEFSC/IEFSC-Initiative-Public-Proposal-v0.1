# IEFSC Initiative

A public proposal for the IEFSC Initiative, an effort to establish the Interactive Entertainment Fair Standards Council.

**Version:** Public Proposal v0.1  
**Date:** May 23, 2026  
**Status:** Early public proposal draft. The IEFSC Initiative is not yet an incorporated organization, government body, or official standards authority.

## Overview

The Interactive Entertainment Fair Standards Council Initiative, or IEFSC Initiative, is a public proposal to establish the Interactive Entertainment Fair Standards Council.

The proposed council would serve as a public-interest advisory and standards body focused on fair standards, consumer transparency, ethical monetization, minors’ protections, digital ownership, live-service shutdowns, preservation, dispute mediation, and long-term industry health across interactive entertainment.

This is not a censorship proposal. The IEFSC would not control creative content, artistic expression, story themes, political viewpoints, humor, game difficulty, genre design, or controversial ideas.

## Current Status

The IEFSC Initiative is not yet an incorporated organization, government body, or official standards authority.

This repository exists as a public record of the proposal and as a place for future drafts, supporting documents, research notes, certification concepts, governance concepts, and standards-development materials.

## Initial Focus Areas

The IEFSC Initiative proposes study and standards development around issues including:

- Predatory monetization
- Loot boxes and randomized paid rewards
- Dark patterns
- Premium currencies
- Psychological spending pressure
- Child and family protections
- Plain-language EULA transparency
- Digital ownership and access
- Live-service shutdowns
- Refund expectations
- Preservation
- Platform accountability
- Consumer expectations
- Complaint resolution
- Dispute mediation between developers, publishers, platforms, and consumers

## Certification Concept

The IEFSC Initiative may later propose voluntary certification standards for companies, platforms, publishers, developers, or products that meet published fair-practice guidelines.

Certification would not mean a company or product is perfect, immune from criticism, or endorsed in every respect. It would simply mean that the certified party meets specific published standards within a defined certification category.

Detailed certification systems, certification codes, audit procedures, and public registry concepts will be developed separately in later documents.

## Governance Concept

The proposed Interactive Entertainment Fair Standards Council would ideally include balanced representation from government, industry, regulatory or technical experts, and ordinary consumers.

Detailed role definitions, appointment procedures, voting procedures, conflict-of-interest rules, and public accountability mechanisms will be developed separately in later documents.
